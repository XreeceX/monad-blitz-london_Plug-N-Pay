# The Story

Dele pulls into a public charging station on his way home from work.

He plugs in. There's no app to open, no card to tap, no "enter payment details" screen. The moment the connector locks in and current starts flowing, a number on his dashboard starts moving — not an estimate, not "you'll be billed later" — an actual balance, ticking down in tiny real increments, once per second, matched exactly to the electrons currently passing through the cable.

He watches it for a moment. Ten seconds of charging, a fraction of a penny gone. He didn't approve a transaction — it's just what's happening, made visible. He only needs a quick top-up today, so he unplugs early. The number simply stops. Not "processing your final bill" — stopped, because nothing is flowing anymore. He never pays for a second he didn't use.

Twenty minutes later, his phone buzzes — his energy provider, offering a premium rate for anyone willing to sell power back tonight, between 6 and 8pm, while the grid is under heavy load. His car's at 80%, more than he needs for tomorrow. He taps yes.

Now the direction reverses. Same connector, same cable — but current now flows *out* of his battery and into the grid, and the same live counter on his dashboard climbs instead of falls. Same mechanism, opposite direction: he's not charging anymore, he's selling, second by second, exactly metered, exactly paid.

No monthly subscription with the charging network. No estimated bill that shows up three weeks later with a dispute process if it's wrong. No middleman batching a thousand people's sessions and settling once a day. Just Dele's car and the station, talking directly, machine to machine, for exactly as long as power is actually moving between them — and the instant it stops, nothing more is owed.

To Dele, charging his car stopped being a bill he receives and became something closer to a live, honest meter — he can see, in real time, exactly what he's using, or exactly what he's earning, down to the second.
